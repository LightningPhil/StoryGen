# Story Crafting Structures: A Multi-Framework Guide

This document provides structured guidance for several popular story crafting methods. Each method includes a description, step-by-step structure, and specific craft rules or tips to help you apply it effectively in your own short stories.

---

## 1. Three-Act Structure

**Overview:**  
A classic and widely used model suitable for most genres and writers.

**Structure:**
1. **Act 1 – Setup:** Introduce characters, setting, and the central problem.
2. **Act 2 – Confrontation:** Develop the conflict with rising action and complications.
3. **Act 3 – Resolution:** Reach a climax and resolve the conflict.

**Craft Tips:**
- Include an inciting incident early in Act 1.
- Ensure Act 2 includes a midpoint twist or major development.
- Show character growth or decision-making in Act 3.

---

## 2. Kishōtenketsu (Japanese Narrative Structure)

**Overview:**  
A conflict-free structure ideal for introspective or emotional stories.

**Structure:**
1. **Ki (Introduction):** Establish characters and world.
2. **Shō (Development):** Develop the situation or character dynamics.
3. **Ten (Twist):** Introduce a surprising or contrasting element.
4. **Ketsu (Conclusion):** Resolve or reframe the narrative based on the twist.

**Craft Tips:**
- Let contrast, not conflict, drive the twist.
- Use visual or emotional motifs to support the shift.
- Works well with moral or theme-based storytelling.

---

## 3. Freytag’s Pyramid

**Overview:**  
Used traditionally in tragedies and dramatic literature.

**Structure:**
1. **Exposition:** Introduce characters and background.
2. **Rising Action:** Complications and events build tension.
3. **Climax:** Turning point of highest tension.
4. **Falling Action:** Events resulting from the climax.
5. **Denouement:** Final resolution or thematic closure.

**Craft Tips:**
- Use symbolic imagery to heighten tension at the climax.
- Ensure falling action feels like consequence, not filler.
- Good for character-driven arcs.

---

## 4. Hero’s Journey (Monomyth)

**Overview:**  
Ideal for epic or transformative character arcs.

**Key Stages:**
1. Ordinary World
2. Call to Adventure
3. Refusal of the Call
4. Meeting the Mentor
5. Crossing the Threshold
6. Tests, Allies, Enemies
7. Approach the Inmost Cave
8. Ordeal
9. Reward
10. The Road Back
11. Resurrection
12. Return with the Elixir

**Craft Tips:**
- Use mentor or guide figures to reflect internal growth.
- Focus on internal change as much as external success.
- Adapt stages for brevity in short stories.

---

## 5. “But, Therefore” Rule

**Overview:**  
A pacing rule from TV writing that creates tight cause-effect storytelling.

**Guideline:**
- Link story beats with **“but…”** (complication) or **“therefore…”** (result).
- Avoid **“and then…”** which weakens causal flow.

**Craft Tips:**
- Use in outlining to ensure logical progression.
- Helps maintain narrative momentum in shorter formats.

---

## 6. Pixar Story Spine

**Overview:**  
A story formula that fosters emotional, child-friendly narratives.

**Structure:**
1. Once upon a time…
2. Every day…
3. Until one day…
4. Because of that…
5. Because of that…
6. Until finally…
7. Ever since then…

**Craft Tips:**
- Keeps the story accessible for young readers.
- Ideal for fables, character arcs, or moral tales.
- “Because of that” beats should escalate naturally.

---

## 7. Chekhov’s Sketch Approach

**Overview:**  
A literary approach focused on quiet moments and subtle transformation.

**Elements:**
- Mood-driven, not plot-driven.
- Endings are reflective or unresolved.
- Character introspection is central.

**Craft Tips:**
- Use physical detail and subtext to reveal inner states.
- Favor implication over exposition.
- Excellent for themes of longing, change, or memory.
